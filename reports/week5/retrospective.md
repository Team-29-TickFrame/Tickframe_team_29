# Sprint Retrospectiv

## What Went Well
•	The team successfully delivered the MVP v2 increment, combining new functionality with maintainability improvements based on customer feedback.
•	Architecture documentation, including static, dynamic, and deployment views, gave the team a clearer understanding of the system structure and simplified technical discussions.
•	Introducing Architecture Decision Records (ADRs) helped document important technical decisions and improved traceability between quality requirements and implementation.
•	Development workflow documentation and configuration management became more structured, making onboarding and future maintenance easier.
•	Continuous integration, automated testing, and code reviews remained part of the regular development workflow and helped maintain product quality.

## What Did Not Go Well
•	Preparing architecture documentation and diagrams required more effort than initially estimated because they had to accurately reflect the current implementation.
•	Some documentation tasks were completed later in the Sprint than planned, increasing the workload near the deadline.
•	Coordinating technical documentation with ongoing implementation required additional communication between team members.
•	The planned customer meeting could not be held during the Sprint because the team member responsible for the major planned product changes was unexpectedly unavailable. As a result, several implementation tasks were delayed and the Sprint Review with the customer had to be postponed until the updated functionality was ready.

## Changes Compared with the Previous Sprint
Based on the previous Sprint Retrospective, the team continued improving its development process:
•	Documentation was maintained throughout the Sprint instead of being created only before submission.
•	Earlier pull request reviews helped identify issues before integration.
•	Customer feedback from MVP v1 was incorporated into Sprint planning and reflected in the MVP v2 scope.
•	The team improved documentation consistency by linking architecture documentation, quality requirements, ADRs, and testing artifacts.

## Process Improvements for the Next Sprint
•	Update architecture documentation immediately after significant implementation changes to keep it synchronized with the codebase.
•	Record Architecture Decision Records as soon as architectural decisions are made instead of documenting them at the end of the Sprint.
•	Continue expanding automated testing for newly implemented features and critical workflows.
•	Allocate dedicated time for reviewing maintained documentation before the Sprint Review to reduce last-minute updates.
•	Continue improving knowledge sharing so that all team members are familiar with key architectural decisions and major product components.
